01 Profile

Proof, then
practice.

A mathematics undergraduate who likes hard questions, clean arguments, and the moment a model finally explains something.

I’m Sicheng Qian, a first-year mathematics student at The Ohio State University. My interests sit where topology meets learning theory — metric spaces, PAC learning, VC dimension, and what it means for a distribution over hypotheses to depend continuously on the data behind it.

At the Math Cycle Program I work through the theory with a Ph.D. mentor, and I have developed a proof of uniform continuity for a learning-algorithm-induced distribution map in the setting we study. It’s the kind of problem where a single counterexample can undo a week of work — which is exactly why I like it.

Alongside the proofs, I founded a society for mathematical modeling and simulation, tutor calculus and statistics, and build software with Python and React — most recently a campus task planner at HackOHI/O, now growing quantum-optimization extensions with IBM Qiskit.
